Quick Buying Guide: What to Look For

When shopping for an entry-level mechanical keyboard, focus on three main specs:

  1. Switch Type:
    • Red Switches (Linear): Quiet, smooth keystrokes. Best for fast-paced gaming and shared offices.
    • Blue Switches (Clicky): Loud, satisfying click sound with physical tactile resistance. Great for typists who like audible feedback.
    • Brown Switches (Tactile): A middle-ground option featuring a silent bump without the loud click sound.
  2. Form Factor:
    • Full-Size (100%): Best for data entry and accounting work requiring a numeric pad.
    • Tenkeyless (TKL / 80%): Removes the numpad to free up desk space while keeping arrow and function keys.
    • 60% Layout: Removes arrows and function rows for maximum portability and mouse room.
  3. Hot-Swappability: Look for boards with hot-swap PCBs, which let you pull switches out and replace them without soldering if one fails or if you want to try different switch types later.
